I build frontends, from the first sketch to the deployed app.

I'm Manan Negi. I design the interface, write the code, and ship it.

You've probably met a dozen people who can open ChatGPT. That's not the skill. The skill is knowing what to put in front of the model, what to leave out, and how to check the output before it reaches a client.

That's what I do. I've been writing code since before AI could write it for me, and I've spent the years since learning exactly how to point these tools at real work.

## Where I came from

Delhi. KVJNU for school. BCA from GGSIPU in 2016, MCA from IGNOU after.

Then Covid hit, and I spent a year in international B2B sales at VSTL, selling bulk PC hardware, new and used, to clients across the Middle East and Europe.

That year wasn't a detour. It taught me how to read a room, handle a hard client, and close. Most developers can't explain what they built to a non-technical buyer. I learned that before I learned React.

## How I learned to build

I left sales and taught myself web development.

Not through a bootcamp. Through building things, breaking them, and reading the errors until the errors stopped repeating.

I joined an AI startup for four or five months. I built landing pages and an app that runs multiple AI providers from one place. That job is where I learned how production apps actually behave, and how fast the ground moves under you when your product depends on models that change every week.

Since then I've been freelancing.

## What I actually do

I design and build:
- Landing pages and marketing sites 
- Web apps, mostly for my own use, which is where I test ideas before I sell them
- Small browser games in Three.js, because they force you to think about performance, interaction, and delight in a way that a CRUD app never does
- This site, which is both my portfolio and a feed of the articles and tools I think are worth your time


## The part that's hard to put on a resume

I know a little about a lot of fields. Design, frontend, backend, AI tooling, prompt structure, agent workflows, 3D on the web. I use AI to close the gap between what I know and what the job needs, fast.

That's not the same as asking a chatbot for code.

I learned to code before AI was good at it. So I know why a function is slow, why a layout breaks, why a dependency is a liability. When the model hands me an answer, I can tell whether it's right, almost right, or confidently wrong. That judgment is the whole game.

The other half is context. Over years of prompting I've learned what a model needs to see: the constraints, the stack, the edge cases, the one detail that changes everything. Give it too little and you get generic slop. Give it too much and it drowns. I've made both mistakes enough times to know the difference.

I also work with agents and skills, the setups that let AI do multi-step work instead of single answers. That's where the leverage is now. Not one prompt, one output. A workflow that holds up when you run it fifty times.

## AI for people who make things

A lot of my work sits between AI and creatives. Designers, artists, illustrators who have the taste and the vision but not the code.

I can take a designer's intent and translate it into something that runs in a browser. Not "make me a website." More like: this is the motion, this is the type scale, this is how it should feel on a phone, now build the thing that matches the picture in their head.

That's a specific skill. It's part taste, part code, part knowing which parts of the job a model should do and which parts it should never touch.

## Staying current is part of the job

I'm on X every day. New models, new tools, new agent frameworks, new ways people are breaking things. I read the release notes, then I test the ones that matter.

Not because it's a hobby. Because in this field, a tool that shipped last month can make your last three months of workflow obsolete.

I've lived that. Before AI got good, I built a tool that placed text behind a person in an image. It was a real product. ChatGPT made it irrelevant in about a week. That's the speed this field moves at, and I'd rather stay close to the edge than pretend it isn't moving.

## How I work

I know a little about a lot of fields, and I use AI to fill the gaps fast.

I don't need to be the expert in everything. I need to know enough to ask the right question, then verify the answer before it ships.

That means:
- I ask for the constraint, not just the feature
- I test the output against the real browser, not the demo
- I keep the code something a human can still read and change later
